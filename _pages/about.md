I am an Assistant Professor of Statistics in the Department of Mathematics, Applied Mathematics, and Statistics at Case Western Reserve University.

My research focuses on the theory and methodology of statistical learning and sequential decision-making. I am particularly interested in contextual bandits, nonparametric statistics, kernel methods, statistical learning theory, and inverse problems, with applications to scientific and societal problems involving uncertainty and data-driven decisions.

## Academic Background

I received my PhD in Statistics from the University of Minnesota, where I was advised by Prof. Yuhong Yang. Before joining Case Western Reserve University, I held postdoctoral positions at Penn State University, the University of Minnesota, and the University of Chicago.

I earned my master's degree in mathematics from the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and my bachelor's degree in mathematics from the University of Delhi.

## Prospective Students

Case Western Reserve University launched its PhD program in Statistics in 2025. Information about the program and application process is available [here](https://mathstats.case.edu/phd-in-statistics/).

I am interested in advising PhD students and collaborating with undergraduate and master's students on research in statistical learning, sequential decision-making, contextual bandits, reinforcement learning, and nonparametric methods.

Students interested in working with me are welcome to email me with a brief description of their research interests and academic background.
